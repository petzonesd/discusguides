Water Quality & Maintenance Routine

By Pet Zone SD Team • September 2026

The Essentials
  • Siphon waste daily to near-daily, especially bare-bottom tanks
  • 25–50% water changes every 1–2 days for serious keepers
  • Rinse filter media in tank water, never tap — protect the bacteria
  • A hose/python system turns maintenance from a chore into a habit
  • Consistency beats intensity — a sustainable routine you keep beats a perfect one you abandon

Maintenance Is the Actual Filter

Water chemistry targets (covered in the water chemistry guide) only hold up if the daily and weekly maintenance routine behind them actually happens. No filter, however oversized, replaces water changes in a discus tank — this is the single most repeated piece of advice in the hobby, and it's repeated because it's genuinely the lever that matters most. This guide is about the mechanics of that routine: what to do, in what order, and how to make it something you'll actually sustain for years, not weeks.

Daily Tasks

  • Siphon visible waste and uneaten food. In a bare-bottom tank this takes under five minutes and should happen daily — waste sitting on a bare floor breaks down fast in 82–86°F water. In a planted or substrate tank, spot-clean visible debris and plan for a more thorough gravel-vac during the weekly water change.
  • Check temperature and observe the group. A quick visual check at feeding time — normal coloring, normal appetite, normal group behavior — catches most problems early, before they show up on a test kit.

The Water Change Itself

Serious keepers change 25–50% of tank volume every one to two days, especially with juveniles, a breeding pair, or a heavily-stocked grow-out tank. A mature, lightly-stocked display can sometimes stretch to every-other-day or twice-weekly large changes, but daily-to-near-daily is the standard for anyone serious about growth rate, color, or breeding success.

  1. Siphon first, then refill. Vacuum waste and debris while draining the percentage you're changing — this combines two jobs into one pass.
  2. Match temperature closely. New water should land within a degree or two of tank temperature; a sharp temperature swing is a real stressor on its own, separate from chemistry.
  3. Treat for chlorine/chloramine every time. Use a dechlorinator rated for chloramine specifically, since most municipal water supplies use it now and discus are notably sensitive to it. Don't let treated water sit around unmixed for long stretches before it goes in.
  4. Refill gently. Pour against a plate, decoration, or the tank glass rather than directly into open water, to avoid startling the group or disturbing substrate.
Make it sustainable

A python-style gravel vacuum/hose system that connects directly to a sink cuts a 50% water change from a 45-minute bucket-hauling chore to a 10-minute one. If your routine depends on carrying buckets, you will eventually skip changes when life gets busy — invest in the hose system early, before that happens, not after a health scare forces the issue.

Filter Maintenance

Rinse mechanical and biological filter media in removed tank water — never tap water — during a water change. Tap water's chlorine or chloramine kills off the beneficial bacteria colony that does the actual biological filtration; rinsing in tank water removes debris while preserving that colony. Stagger filter media cleaning (don't deep-clean every media type on the same day) so the bacterial colony never takes a hit all at once.

Replace mechanical filter floss/pads as they clog and restrict flow, but treat biological media (ceramic rings, bio-balls, sponge) as long-term residents that get rinsed, not replaced, except when they're physically breaking down.

Weekly / Periodic Tasks

  • Full parameter test — pH, ammonia, nitrite, nitrate — even in an established tank, to catch slow drift before it becomes a problem.
  • Glass and hardscape cleaning — algae scraping and wiping down décor, mainly cosmetic but also prevents biofilm buildup in hard-to-reach corners.
  • Equipment check — verify heater accuracy against a separate thermometer, and confirm filter flow hasn't dropped from clogging.

Building a Routine You'll Actually Keep

The honest failure mode in discus keeping isn't misunderstanding the chemistry — it's a routine that's too ambitious to sustain once life gets busy, which then lapses for a week, which is exactly when hole-in-the-head disease or a dark, stressed group shows up. Set a realistic default (most keepers land on every-other-day 25–30% changes as a sustainable long-term baseline, stepping up to daily during breeding or juvenile grow-out) and protect that routine with the right tools — a hose system, pre-treated water storage, a testing kit kept somewhere visible — rather than relying on willpower alone.
