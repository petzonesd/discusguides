Discus Care Fundamentals

Everything you need to keep your discus healthy and thriving.

Care fundamentals are universal across discus strains — the color and pattern differences covered in the variety library don't change any of the water, feeding, or maintenance requirements below. Master these, and you're most of the way to a thriving tank.

If you're setting up your first discus tank, read in this order:

  1. Tank Setup — physical foundation, tall tank, substrate decision
  2. Water Parameters — temperature, pH, hardness targets
  3. Water Quality Monitoring — the maintenance routine that actually matters most
  4. Feeding — nutrition and growth

Then, once your tank is established and fish are in, read Common Diseases and Social Dynamics so you can recognize problems before they spiral.

The Non-Negotiables

If you remember nothing else, remember this:

  • Discus need warm water — 82–86°F — held steady, not most tropical community temperatures.
  • Water changes are the real filter. Serious keepers change 25–50% every one to two days, especially with juveniles.
  • Discus are shoaling fish. Keep six or more, never a single fish or an unbuffered pair outside breeding.
  • Tall tanks matter as much as gallons — discus are deep-bodied and need vertical room.
  • Ammonia and nitrite must read zero, always. Discus show the effects of lapses here faster than most fish.

Get these five things right, and everything else becomes easier.

Discus Care FAQ

What temperature do discus need?

82–86°F (28–30°C) — well above what most tropical community fish tolerate long-term, which is the main reason discus get a species-only tank. See water chemistry for the full parameter breakdown.

How often should I change the water?

Serious keepers change 25–50% of tank volume every one to two days, especially with juveniles or a breeding pair. No filter, however large, replaces this routine — details in water quality & maintenance.

What do discus eat?

A quality discus-formulated pellet as the staple, supplemented with beef heart mix, frozen bloodworms, and live foods for variety and conditioning. Full schedule in the feeding guide.

Why are my discus turning dark?

Usually a stress symptom rather than a disease — new water, an undersized group, aggressive tankmates, or a chemistry swing. Test water first; see common diseases and social dynamics for how to tell the two apart.
