Small tank, unforgiving standards

Discus have a reputation, and it's earned. They're not hard because they're delicate specimens that keel over at the first mistake — they're hard because they demand consistency. Warm water, low organics, and steady chemistry, held for years, not weeks. Get it right and you get a fish that schools, pair-bonds, feeds its own fry off its skin, and grows into a dinner-plate-sized centerpiece with color that shifts by the week.

Discus Fish FAQ

Are discus fish hard to keep?

They're not fragile, but they are demanding — the difficulty is almost entirely about consistency, not delicacy. Discus need warmer water than most tropical fish (82–86°F), frequent water changes, and a real commitment to water testing. Get those habits in place and discus are genuinely manageable; skip them and problems show up fast. See care fundamentals for the full breakdown.

How big do discus fish get?

Most farm-raised strains reach 6–8 inches (15–20 cm) in diameter as adults, though wild-caught fish tend to run a bit smaller, closer to 5–7 inches. Adult size and body depth both depend heavily on genetics, water quality, and diet during the juvenile growth stage.

How long do discus fish live?

A well-kept discus typically lives 10–15 years, sometimes longer with excellent water quality and diet. Lifespan tracks husbandry closely — stunted growth or chronic water-quality issues early in life usually shorten it.

What size tank do discus need?

55 gallons is the realistic floor for a small group of four to six juveniles, with 75–125 gallons recommended for a full-grown group of six adults. Tank height matters as much as footprint — discus are tall, deep-bodied fish and want at least 20 inches of vertical space. Full detail in tank setup.

Can discus live with other fish?

Yes — discus are peaceful toward other species, with almost all aggression concerns being between discus themselves as they sort out a pecking order. Cardinal tetras, rummynose tetras, corydoras, and otocinclus are classic, compatible tankmates. See social dynamics for what to avoid.

What's the best discus strain for a beginner?

Red Turquoise is the standard starting point — it's the most widely farmed, most affordable, and most forgiving of the color strains, and it teaches you the fundamentals before you invest in rarer stock. See the full variety library to compare strains.
