Snakeskin Discus

By Pet Zone SD Team • September 2026

The Essentials
  • Size: 6–8 inches (15–20 cm) as adults
  • Tank: 55 gallons minimum for a group, tall (20"+ height)
  • pH: 6.0–7.2
  • Temperature: 82–86°F (28–30°C)
  • Hardness: Soft to moderate (2–8 dGH)
  • Temperament: Peaceful, shoaling
  • Difficulty: Intermediate

Overview

Snakeskin is defined by pattern rather than color — a fine, netted webbing of lines across the entire body that looks etched rather than painted on, distinct from the broader, looser striping of Red Turquoise. The "snakeskin" pattern gene has become a popular base for hybridizing with other color strains, so you'll increasingly see it combined with red or blue base tones rather than as a standalone white-and-black pattern fish — the Blue Snakeskin color form is one of the most popular results, and one of Pet Zone's best sellers.

Body depth and finnage tend to track whatever base strain the snakeskin pattern was crossed onto, so quality varies more by breeder and lineage than with a strain like Blue Diamond that's been standardized longer.

Tank Setup & Water

No different from other discus strains — see the Red Turquoise guide for the complete tank, substrate, and water chemistry breakdown (55 gallons minimum, tall tank, pH 6.0–7.2, 82–86°F). Even, non-glaring lighting shows the fine pattern detail best; overly bright or blue-heavy aquascape lighting can wash it out, flattening the contrast that makes the netting visible.

Feeding

Standard discus diet — quality pellets as a staple with beef heart mix, bloodworms, and live foods in rotation for growth and conditioning, per the feeding guide. No pattern-specific dietary needs; a well-fed fish simply shows its netting more crisply against healthy skin.

Temperament & Compatibility

Standard discus shoaling behavior — peaceful with tankmates, group-dependent for security and reduced intraspecific aggression, and fully compatible with the usual cardinal tetra / corydoras / rummynose tetra / otocinclus lineup covered in the Red Turquoise guide.

Breeding Notes

Standard pairing, spawning, and parental slime-feeding behavior — see the breeding guide for the full walkthrough. Because snakeskin is frequently crossed onto other base strains rather than fixed as its own pure line, fry from a home pairing can show a wide spread of pattern intensity and base color, from crisp full netting down to faint or partial expression.

Common Issues

No pattern-specific health issues — Snakeskin is subject to the same general discus concerns (hole-in-the-head, stunting, stress-related dulling) covered in common diseases. A fading or blotchy-looking pattern is more often a lighting or water-clarity issue than a health one; clear, well-maintained water is what lets the fine netting read clearly in the first place.

Where to Get Quality Fish

Look for crisp, well-defined netting across the whole body rather than patchy or fading pattern, and ask about the base strain it was crossed onto if color is a priority — that base strain affects body depth and finnage as much as the pattern gene does. Pet Zone SD stocks Snakeskin and its popular Blue Snakeskin color form when available. See our sourcing guide for more.

Cost: Typically $20–40 for quality juveniles, in line with other common farm strains.

Final Thoughts

Snakeskin is a good pick for a keeper who wants something visually distinct from the classic Red Turquoise look without taking on any additional care difficulty — it's the same fish underneath a different pattern.

Snakeskin FAQ

What makes Snakeskin discus different from other strains?

It's defined by pattern rather than color — a fine, dense, wavy-line netting across the whole body that looks etched rather than painted on, distinct from the broader striping of Red Turquoise. It's usually crossed onto another base color rather than kept as a standalone look.

What's the difference between Snakeskin and Blue Snakeskin discus?

Same pattern gene, different base color. Blue Snakeskin expresses the fine netting over a cool blue base rather than the red or gold tones the pattern is more commonly crossed onto.

Is Snakeskin discus hard to care for?

No harder than any other discus strain. Difficulty comes from being a discus at all — warm water, frequent water changes, shoaling — not from the snakeskin pattern itself.
