Snakeskin Discus

By Pet Zone SD Team • September 2026

The Essentials
  • Size: 6–8 inches (15–20 cm) as adults
  • Tank: 55 gallons minimum for a group, tall (20"+ height)
  • pH: 6.0–7.2
  • Temperature: 82–86°F (28–30°C)
  • Hardness: Soft to moderate (2–8 dGH)
  • Temperament: Peaceful, shoaling
  • Difficulty: Intermediate

Overview

Snakeskin is defined by pattern rather than color — a fine, netted webbing of lines across the entire body that looks etched rather than painted on, distinct from the broader, looser striping of Red Turquoise. The "snakeskin" pattern gene has become a popular base for hybridizing with other color strains, so you'll increasingly see it combined with red or blue base tones rather than as a standalone white-and-black pattern fish.

Body depth and finnage tend to track whatever base strain the snakeskin pattern was crossed onto, so quality varies more by breeder and lineage than with a strain like Blue Diamond that's been standardized longer.

Tank Setup & Water

No different from other discus strains — see the Red Turquoise guide for the complete tank, substrate, and water chemistry breakdown. Even, non-glaring lighting shows the fine pattern detail best; overly bright or blue-heavy aquascape lighting can wash it out.

Feeding

Standard discus diet — quality pellets as a staple with beef heart mix, bloodworms, and live foods in rotation for growth and conditioning.

Temperament & Compatibility

Standard discus shoaling behavior — peaceful with tankmates, group-dependent for security and reduced intraspecific aggression.

Breeding Notes

Standard pairing and parental slime-feeding behavior — see the Red Turquoise guide for the full walkthrough. Because snakeskin is frequently crossed onto other base strains, fry from a home pairing can show a wide spread of pattern intensity.

Where to Get Quality Fish

Look for crisp, well-defined netting across the whole body rather than patchy or fading pattern, and ask about the base strain it was crossed onto if color is a priority. See our sourcing guide for more.

Final Thoughts

Snakeskin is a good pick for a keeper who wants something visually distinct from the classic Red Turquoise look without taking on any additional care difficulty — it's the same fish underneath a different pattern.
