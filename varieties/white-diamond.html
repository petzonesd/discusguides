White Diamond Discus

By Pet Zone SD Team • September 2026

The Essentials
  • Size: 6–8 inches (15–20 cm) as adults
  • Tank: 55 gallons minimum for a group, tall (20"+ height)
  • pH: 6.0–7.2
  • Temperature: 82–86°F (28–30°C)
  • Hardness: Soft to moderate (2–8 dGH)
  • Temperament: Peaceful, shoaling
  • Difficulty: Intermediate to advanced

Overview

White Diamond is a solid-color farm strain bred for a clean, pearlescent white body — visually striking against darker planted or hardscaped tanks, and a genuine standout in a mixed-strain display. The key identifying detail, and the one that separates it from the true Albino strain covered elsewhere on this site, is the eye: White Diamond retains normal, dark-pigmented eyes, while true albino discus have red or pink eyes from a lack of eye pigment. The body color can look similar at a glance, so this is the detail to check if a seller's labeling seems uncertain.

Like other solid-color strains, White Diamond is judged on evenness and cleanliness of color — any yellowish tinge, patchiness, or visible blemishing reads as lower quality, and also tends to be one of the more visible signs of stress or poor water quality in this particular strain.

Tank Setup & Water

  • Tank: Standard discus baseline — tall, 55 gallons minimum for a growing group.
  • Substrate: A darker substrate or background makes the white coloring pop considerably more than a pale or bright-white tank background.
  • Water chemistry: Standard discus range; as with other solid-color strains, stable water helps keep the color clean rather than yellowed or blotchy.

Feeding

Standard discus diet — quality pellets as the staple, with beef heart mix and frozen foods in rotation, per the feeding guide. Unlike red or orange strains, White Diamond doesn't particularly benefit from carotenoid-heavy color foods; the goal here is clean, even white rather than deepened pigment.

Temperament & Compatibility

No different from other discus strains — peaceful, shoaling, best kept in groups of six or more, and fully compatible with the usual Amazon-biotope tankmate lineup. Reads as an especially strong visual contrast fish alongside darker or more saturated strains like Marlboro Red or Blue Turquoise.

Breeding Notes

Standard discus pairing, spawning, and parental slime-feeding — see the breeding guide for the full cycle. Because White Diamond and Albino can carry related genetics, a breeding program mixing the two strains can produce mixed or unexpected offspring; keep lines separate if you're breeding for a specific look.

Common Issues

Yellowing or Dulling

Cause: Almost always tannin staining or declining water quality rather than illness, given how visible it is against the white ground color — similar to how Pigeon Blood's pale body shows the same problem.

Fix: Step up water change frequency and check for tannin sources. Persistent dulling despite clean water is worth investigating via common diseases.

Where to Get Quality Fish

Confirm eye color when buying to be sure you're getting White Diamond rather than Albino if the distinction matters for your plans, and look for clean, even white with no yellow tinge or patchiness. Pet Zone SD stocks White Diamond when available. See our sourcing guide for general buying advice.

Cost: Typically $20–40 for quality juveniles, in line with other common farm strains.

Final Thoughts

White Diamond is a clean, elegant solid-color strain that rewards the same water-quality discipline as any other discus — a genuinely striking pick for a keeper who wants brightness and contrast in a mixed-strain display tank.

White Diamond FAQ

What's the difference between White Diamond and Albino discus?

Eye color. White Diamond retains normal, dark-pigmented eyes; true Albino discus have red or pink eyes from a lack of eye pigment. The body color can look similar at a glance, so eye color is the reliable way to tell them apart.

Why is my White Diamond discus turning yellow?

A yellowish tinge on a White Diamond is usually a water-quality issue — tannin staining, nitrate buildup, or general decline — rather than illness. Clean, stable water is what keeps the white looking genuinely clean rather than dingy.

Do White Diamond and Albino discus breed true if crossed?

Not reliably. Because the two strains can carry related underlying genetics, crossing them can produce mixed or unexpected offspring — keep lines separate if you're breeding for a specific look.
