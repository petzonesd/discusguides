Rose Red Discus

By Pet Zone SD Team • September 2026

The Essentials
  • Size: 6–8 inches (15–20 cm) as adults
  • Tank: 55 gallons minimum for a group, tall (20"+ height)
  • pH: 6.0–7.2
  • Temperature: 82–86°F (28–30°C)
  • Hardness: Soft to moderate (2–8 dGH)
  • Temperament: Peaceful, shoaling
  • Difficulty: Intermediate to advanced — solid color shows stress and poor water fastest

Overview

Rose Red sits in the same solid-color breeding tradition as Marlboro Red and Melon Red, but grades out softer and pinker — a rosy, slightly muted red rather than Marlboro's deep, saturated brick tone. It's frequently sold alongside those two under the broader "solid red" umbrella, and the three are functionally identical to keep; the differences are cosmetic grading, not separate husbandry categories.

Like any solid-color strain, Rose Red is judged almost entirely on evenness and saturation rather than pattern definition, which makes it an honest barometer of water quality and diet — a stressed or poorly fed Rose Red dulls and patches visibly, well before a patterned strain would show the same stress.

Tank Setup & Water

No different from other discus strains — see the Red Turquoise guide for the complete tank, substrate, and water chemistry breakdown. Bare-bottom setups are common among keepers chasing peak, even color, since they make the water-quality discipline this strain rewards much easier to maintain.

Feeding

Standard discus diet — quality pellets as a staple, with beef heart mix and color-enhancing, carotenoid-rich foods in rotation to support red saturation.

Temperament & Compatibility

Standard discus shoaling behavior — peaceful, group-dependent, no special compatibility concerns beyond the usual discus tankmate guidance.

Breeding Notes

Standard pairing and parental slime-feeding — see the Red Turquoise guide for the full walkthrough. Solid-color genetics breed comparatively true, though achieving uniformly even, show-quality red across an entire spawn still takes selective culling over generations.

Where to Get Quality Fish

Look for smooth, even coloring with no patchiness or pale blotches, and clean, undamaged fins — the same visual checks that apply to any solid-color strain. See our sourcing guide for more.

Final Thoughts

Rose Red is a good pick for a keeper who likes the solid-color look but wants something a shade softer than Marlboro Red's intensity — the care is identical, and the payoff is a genuinely striking, low-pattern centerpiece fish.
