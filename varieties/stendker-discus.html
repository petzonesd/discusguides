Stendker Discus

By Pet Zone SD Team • October 2026

The Essentials
  • What it is: A breeding line/brand (dozens of color variants), not a strain name like Red Turquoise
  • Origin: Diskuszucht Stendker, Warendorf, Germany — founded 1965
  • Size: 6–7 inches (15–18 cm) as adults
  • Tank: 55–75 gallons+ for a juvenile group
  • pH: 6.5–7.5 — notably tolerant of neutral, harder water
  • Temperature: 82–86°F (28–30°C)
  • Hardness: Tolerates moderately hard tap water, unlike soft-water-dependent strains
  • Difficulty: Beginner-friendly for a discus — the main reason to seek this line out

What "Stendker" Actually Means

Every other page in this library covers a color or pattern — Red Turquoise, Blue Diamond, Checkerboard. Stendker is different: it's a breeding line named after Diskuszucht Stendker GmbH & Co. KG, a family-run discus farm in Warendorf, North Rhine-Westphalia, that's produced fish across dozens of color variants since 1965. When a retailer lists "Stendker discus" or "Stendker-line" stock, they're telling you about the breeding program and philosophy behind the fish, not a specific color — the actual fish might be a Red Turquoise, a White Leopard, a Cobalt, or any of the two dozen-plus variants the farm has worked with.

That distinction matters because it changes what the label is actually promising you. A "Stendker Red Turquoise" and a generic Asian-farm Red Turquoise can look nearly identical in a photo, but the Stendker name is a claim about the breeding and rearing conditions behind the fish — specifically, selection for hardiness and tolerance of harder, more neutral water than discus typically get credit for handling.

Why the Line Exists — Breeding for Tap Water

Most discus care advice, including most of this site's own water-chemistry guidance, leans toward soft, slightly acidic water because that's what the wild Amazon basin populations (and many farm strains bred to resemble them) respond best to. Stendker took a different approach: the farm raises its fish in relatively hard, neutral water — reported around pH 7 and warm temperatures — rather than softened or blackwater conditions, on the theory that a fish raised in harder water from the start adapts better to the tap water most hobbyists actually have, rather than needing RO/DI remineralization to match a softer ideal.

In practice, that means Stendker-line fish are commonly recommended as a more forgiving on-ramp for a keeper without an RO/DI setup — dechlorinated tap water in most municipal supplies (roughly 6.5–7.5 pH, moderate hardness) falls within what the line tolerates, where a softer-water-dependent strain might struggle long-term on the same water. It doesn't remove any of the other demands discus make — see water chemistry and the maintenance routine for what still applies regardless of lineage — but it removes one common barrier to entry.

The Proprietary Diet

Stendker also became known for its own feed line — a beef-heart-based "Good Heart" frozen mix and granulated dry foods formulated specifically for discus nutrition and color development. The farm has continued manufacturing and selling these foods even as the fish-breeding side of the business has gone through disruption (below), and they're worth knowing about by name if you're sourcing discus food from a specialty supplier. They're a supplement to, not a replacement for, the varied diet covered in our own feeding guide.

A Line in Flux: What's Actually Happening in Germany

This is worth spelling out clearly because sources genuinely disagree on the current state of things, and a lot of older content online hasn't caught up. In mid-2022, Diskuszucht Stendker announced that rising energy costs had made its Warendorf breeding operation financially unsustainable and signaled it would wind down production by the end of that year. Around the same time, the company also announced "Stendker 3.0" — a generational handover to Sebastian Stendker, continuing the family's third generation at the helm — which kept the Warendorf farm breeding discus past the 2022 scare; the company's own site has continued posting farm updates since.

More recently, a separate US operation — Stendker Discus USA, a sister business to Super Cichlids in Delaware — has described itself as stepping in as the German side's output has tightened, breeding Stendker-line fish domestically out of a dedicated facility. The two accounts (an ongoing, updated German farm vs. a handover to a US successor) aren't fully reconcilable from public information as of this writing. The practical takeaway for buyers: don't assume "Stendker" on a label means fish imported directly from Warendorf — ask your supplier specifically whether the stock is German-bred, US-bred, or a Stendker-line descendant raised by a smaller breeder working from earlier stock, since all three are circulating under some version of the name right now.

Care Notes Beyond the Water Chemistry Edge

Outside of the harder-water tolerance, a Stendker-line discus is still a discus, and every other care fundamental on this site applies without exception: the same 82–86°F temperature range, the same shoaling requirement (six or more, never kept alone or as an unbuffered pair outside breeding), the same tall-tank preference, and the same expectation of frequent partial water changes rather than relying on filtration alone. See care fundamentals for the full rundown.

New imports — whichever source farm they came from — benefit from a patient quarantine period before joining an established group: several weeks in a bare, easily-monitored tank, watching for the stress-related issues covered in common diseases, is standard practice for any freshly-imported discus and applies just as much here.

Should You Seek Out Stendker Specifically?

If you're a first-time discus keeper without an RO/DI setup and you can confirm a supplier's stock actually traces to the Stendker breeding program (ask directly — the name gets used loosely), it's one of the more beginner-friendly entry points into the hobby for exactly the reason above. If you already run RO/DI and dial in soft water as a matter of course, the water-tolerance advantage matters less, and strain choice (covered throughout the rest of this library, starting with Red Turquoise) becomes the bigger factor in what to buy.

Stendker Discus FAQ

What is Stendker discus?

Not a color strain — Stendker is a breeding line, named after Diskuszucht Stendker, a family-run German discus farm founded in 1965 in Warendorf, North Rhine-Westphalia. The farm has produced dozens of color variants over six decades, selectively bred for hardiness and tolerance of harder, tap-based water rather than soft blackwater.

Is Stendker discus still being bred in Germany?

It's genuinely unsettled. The German farm announced in 2022 that rising energy costs made production unsustainable, then underwent a generational handover ("Stendker 3.0") that kept the Warendorf operation running; more recent reporting describes a US-based partner facility taking up breeding as German output has tightened. Confirm with your specific supplier what you're actually buying.

What makes Stendker discus different from other discus?

Deliberate selection for tolerance of harder, more neutral water rather than the soft, acidic conditions some discus need, plus a proprietary diet (Good Heart beef-heart mix and granulated feed) the farm still manufactures and sells. The result is a reputation as one of the more forgiving import lines for a keeper on ordinary tap water.

Can I keep Stendker discus in regular tap water?

That's the line's main selling point — it was bred in moderately hard, near-neutral water (roughly pH 6.5–7.5), so dechlorinated tap water in most municipal supplies falls within tolerance without RO/DI treatment. The usual temperature, shoaling, and water-change requirements covered in our care guides still apply in full.
