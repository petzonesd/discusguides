Checkerboard Discus

By Pet Zone SD Team • September 2026

The Essentials
  • Size: 6–8 inches (15–20 cm) as adults
  • Tank: 55 gallons minimum for a group, tall (20"+ height)
  • pH: 6.0–7.2
  • Temperature: 82–86°F (28–30°C)
  • Hardness: Soft to moderate (2–8 dGH)
  • Temperament: Peaceful, shoaling
  • Difficulty: Intermediate

Overview

Checkerboard is a pattern strain defined by a broken, tiled arrangement of color blocks across the body — a look that reads almost geometric compared to the flowing stripes of Red Turquoise or the fine netting of Snakeskin. It's less consistently available at general retail than the "essential three" strains, which makes it more of a pattern-collector's pick than a typical first discus.

As with Snakeskin, the checkerboard pattern gene is often combined with other base colors rather than kept as a standalone look, so you'll see meaningful variation in exactly how the tiling reads from one line to the next. At retail, the name also gets applied loosely to the vivid Yellow Pigeon strain — sold by some sellers as "Yellow Checkerboard" — which is genetically a pigeon-pattern fish rather than a true Checkerboard.

Tank Setup & Water

No different from other discus strains — see the Red Turquoise guide for the complete tank, substrate, and water chemistry breakdown (55 gallons minimum, tall tank, pH 6.0–7.2, 82–86°F). Even lighting shows the tiled pattern's contrast best without washing it toward either solid-looking red or white.

Feeding

Standard discus diet — quality pellets as a staple with beef heart mix, bloodworms, and live foods in rotation, per the feeding guide. No pattern-specific dietary needs.

Temperament & Compatibility

Standard discus shoaling behavior — peaceful, group-dependent, happiest in six or more, with no special compatibility concerns beyond the usual discus tankmate guidance in the Red Turquoise guide.

Breeding Notes

Standard pairing, spawning, and parental slime-feeding — see the breeding guide for the full walkthrough. Pattern strains generally produce more variable-looking fry than solid-color strains, so a home-bred spawn will show a spread of pattern expression rather than a uniform tiled look across the whole brood.

Common Issues

No pattern-specific health concerns — Checkerboard is subject to the same general discus issues covered in common diseases. Because it's less standardized than mainstream strains, body depth and finnage can vary more noticeably between individual fish; that's a cosmetic quality issue rather than a health one.

Where to Get Quality Fish

Because it's less commonly stocked, sourcing from a dedicated discus breeder or specialty importer is more reliable than waiting on general retail availability. Be specific about wanting true Checkerboard rather than Yellow Pigeon, since the two are sometimes conflated at retail under the "checkerboard" name. See our sourcing guide for more.

Cost: Often runs somewhat higher than the "essential three" strains given its lower availability — expect to pay a premium over standard Red Turquoise or Blue Diamond pricing for a clean, well-defined specimen.

Final Thoughts

Checkerboard is a good pick once you've kept a more common strain and want something visually distinct — the care is identical, the pattern is the whole appeal, and tracking down a good one is part of the fun for a pattern-focused collector.

Checkerboard FAQ

Is Yellow Pigeon the same as Checkerboard discus?

No, though some sellers market Yellow Pigeon as "Yellow Checkerboard." True Checkerboard is genetically its own broken, tiled pattern strain; Yellow Pigeon is a pigeon-pattern fish expressed as vivid yellow. Ask a seller which one they actually mean before buying.

Why is Checkerboard discus harder to find?

It's less standardized and less heavily farmed at scale than strains like Red Turquoise or Blue Diamond, making it more of a pattern-collector's strain sourced through dedicated breeders or specialty importers rather than typical general retail.

Is Checkerboard discus harder to care for than other strains?

No — care is identical to any discus strain. The pattern gene affects appearance only; water, feeding, and tank requirements don't change.
