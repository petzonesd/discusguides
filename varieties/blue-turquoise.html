Blue Turquoise Discus

By Pet Zone SD Team • September 2026

The Essentials
  • Size: 6–8 inches (15–20 cm) as adults
  • Tank: 55 gallons minimum for a group, tall (20"+ height)
  • pH: 6.0–7.2
  • Temperature: 82–86°F (28–30°C)
  • Hardness: Soft to moderate (2–8 dGH)
  • Temperament: Peaceful, shoaling
  • Difficulty: Intermediate to advanced

Overview

Blue Turquoise is a near-solid-color strain built around brilliant, electric blue iridescence covering most of the body, typically with light red spotting or striping retained along the dorsal fin and face as a secondary accent. It's worth distinguishing clearly from the flagship Red Turquoise strain covered elsewhere on this site: Red Turquoise is a turquoise base heavily overlaid with red striations across the whole body, while Blue Turquoise pushes the blue itself to near-solid coverage and keeps the red as a minor accent rather than a defining pattern.

The iridescent blue in this strain is genuinely one of the most striking colors in the discus hobby under the right lighting — it can shift from a deep cobalt to a bright electric cyan depending on angle and light temperature, which is part of why turquoise-based strains remain some of the most popular in the hobby generations after their initial development.

Tank Setup & Water

  • Tank: Standard discus baseline — tall, 55 gallons minimum for a growing group.
  • Substrate: Either works; a darker substrate or background makes the electric blue pop noticeably more than a bright or pale tank.
  • Water chemistry: Standard discus range — 6.0–7.2 pH, 82–86°F.

Feeding

Standard discus diet — quality pellets as the staple, with beef heart mix and frozen foods in rotation. Unlike red or orange strains, Blue Turquoise doesn't benefit meaningfully from carotenoid-heavy foods; consistent, high-quality nutrition and stable water do more for iridescent blue tone than any specific color-boosting ingredient.

Temperament & Compatibility

Standard discus social behavior — peaceful, shoaling, best kept in groups of six or more. Reads as a strong cool-toned contrast alongside warmer strains like Melon or Lemon Yellow in a mixed display.

Breeding Notes

Standard discus pairing, spawning, and parental slime-feeding — see the Red Turquoise guide for the full cycle. Turquoise-family genetics are well established in the hobby and breed reasonably true, though the balance between blue coverage and red accenting varies across a spawn.

Where to Get Quality Fish

Look for bright, even blue iridescence without dull or patchy sections, and confirm with the seller whether the fish is graded as Blue Turquoise or the more heavily red-patterned Red Turquoise if the distinction matters to you. See our sourcing guide for general buying advice.

Final Thoughts

Blue Turquoise is a showstopping strain for a keeper who wants the iridescent blue that made turquoise discus famous, without the heavier red patterning of the classic Red Turquoise look — a strong centerpiece choice once your discus fundamentals are solid.
