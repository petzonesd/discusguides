Yellow Pigeon Discus

By Pet Zone SD Team • September 2026

The Essentials
  • Size: 6–8 inches (15–20 cm) as adults; young stock often sold around 3–4 inches
  • Tank: 55 gallons minimum for a group, tall (20"+ height)
  • pH: 6.0–7.2
  • Temperature: 82–86°F (28–30°C)
  • Hardness: Soft to moderate (2–8 dGH)
  • Temperament: Peaceful, shoaling
  • Difficulty: Intermediate

Overview

Yellow Pigeon is a vividly colored strain built on the same reduced-pigment "pigeon" pattern gene behind Pigeon Blood, but expressed here as a bright, vivid yellow rather than Pigeon Blood's pale, red-flecked look. It's also commonly sold as the Yellow Checkerboard discus, since the pigeon pattern's broken markings can read as a loose, tiled pattern similar to true Checkerboard — the two strains are genetically distinct, but the names get used interchangeably at retail, so don't be surprised to see either label on the same fish.

It's consistently one of the more eye-catching discus available, and the pale-to-bright yellow ground color makes it a strong visual contrast piece in a display tank alongside deeper-red or blue strains.

Tank Setup & Water

No different from other discus strains — see the Red Turquoise guide for the complete tank, substrate, and water chemistry breakdown (55 gallons minimum, tall tank, pH 6.0–7.2, 82–86°F). A mid-tone or darker background makes the bright yellow pop without washing it out under standard aquarium lighting.

Feeding

Standard discus diet — quality pellets as a staple with beef heart mix, bloodworms, and live foods in rotation for growth and conditioning, per the feeding guide. Spirulina-inclusive foods are sometimes credited with supporting a brighter yellow tone.

Temperament & Compatibility

Standard discus shoaling behavior — peaceful with tankmates, group-dependent for security and reduced intraspecific aggression. Its bright yellow tone makes a strong contrast piece alongside deeper-red or blue strains in a mixed-strain display.

Breeding Notes

Standard pairing, spawning, and parental slime-feeding — see the breeding guide for the full walkthrough. Like other pigeon-pattern strains, inheritance is more variable than solid-color genetics, so home-bred fry won't reliably all match the parents' color intensity — expect a spread across a spawn.

Common Issues

No strain-specific health concerns beyond the general discus issues covered in common diseases. A dull or washed-out yellow that wasn't there at purchase points more toward stress or water quality than illness.

Where to Get Quality Fish

Look for bright, even yellow with no dull or washed-out patches, and clear, undamaged fins. Ask whether a seller is using "Yellow Pigeon" or "Yellow Checkerboard" for the same fish, since the names overlap at retail. Pet Zone SD stocks Yellow Pigeon when available. See our sourcing guide for more.

Cost: Typically $20–40 for quality juveniles, in line with other common farm strains.

Final Thoughts

Yellow Pigeon is a great choice for a keeper who wants a genuinely bright, non-red centerpiece fish — the care is identical to any other farm-strain discus, and the color payoff is one of the most vivid in the hobby.

Yellow Pigeon FAQ

Is Yellow Pigeon the same as Yellow Checkerboard discus?

At retail, yes — the two names are used interchangeably for the same fish. Genetically, Yellow Pigeon is built on the pigeon pattern gene, while true Checkerboard is a separate, distinct pattern strain; the names overlap because pigeon's broken markings can read as a loose tiled pattern similar to Checkerboard.

What's the difference between Yellow Pigeon and Pigeon Blood discus?

Both share the same underlying pigeon pattern gene. Pigeon Blood expresses it as pale white to pigeon-grey with red stippling; Yellow Pigeon expresses the same gene as a vivid yellow base instead.

Will Yellow Pigeon discus fry match their parents' color?

Not reliably. Like other pigeon-pattern strains, inheritance is more variable than solid-color genetics, so home-bred fry won't all match the parents' color intensity or pattern.
