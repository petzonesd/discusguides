Blue Diamond Discus

By Pet Zone SD Team • September 2026

The Essentials
  • Size: 6–8 inches (15–20 cm) as adults
  • Tank: 55 gallons minimum for a group, tall (20"+ height)
  • pH: 6.0–7.2
  • Temperature: 82–86°F (28–30°C)
  • Hardness: Soft to moderate (2–8 dGH)
  • Temperament: Peaceful, shoaling
  • Difficulty: Intermediate to advanced — solid color shows stress and poor water fastest

Overview

Blue Diamond is one of the benchmark solid-color discus strains — bred for an even, near-metallic blue that covers the entire body with minimal secondary patterning. It's a genuinely different breeding project from patterned strains like Red Turquoise: where turquoise-and-red strains are judged on contrast and pattern definition, solid strains like Blue Diamond are judged almost entirely on evenness, saturation, and the absence of blemishes. That makes it an unforgiving strain to grade, and a satisfying one to keep well.

The trade-off for that clean, uniform look is that solid-color strains tend to show stress, poor diet, and declining water quality faster and more visibly than patterned fish — a stressed Blue Diamond dulls and blotches in a way that's immediately obvious, where a patterned strain can hide the same stress longer. Treat that visibility as a diagnostic advantage: this fish will tell you when something's wrong before it becomes serious, often days before a patterned tankmate would show the same problem.

Like every farm strain in the hobby, Blue Diamond descends from the wild Symphysodon aequifasciatus/haraldi complex, pushed over generations of selective breeding toward a single, saturated, solid-blue result instead of the wild fish's banded, mottled camouflage. The name "diamond" refers to the metallic, almost crystalline sheen good specimens carry under aquarium lighting — not a literal diamond pattern.

Identifying Quality Blue Diamond

Because there's no pattern to hide behind, Blue Diamond is graded almost entirely on three things: evenness of color across the whole body with no pale patches or dark blotching, depth and metallic saturation of the blue itself (flat, washed-out blue reads as lower quality than a rich, almost iridescent tone), and body condition — deep-bodied, undamaged fins, and confident posture. A faint ghost of the wild-type vertical barring showing through under stress or at rest is normal in even good specimens and isn't itself a quality flaw; barring that never fades even in a calm, settled tank is worth asking a seller about.

Tank Setup & Water

Baseline tank requirements track Red Turquoise closely — see the Red Turquoise guide for the full breakdown of tank sizing, hardscape, and filtration. A few things are worth calling out specifically for Blue Diamond:

  • Tank: 55 gallons minimum for a group of four to six juveniles growing out, moving toward 75–125 gallons as adults reach 6–8 inches. Height matters as much as footprint — 20 inches or taller.
  • Substrate: Bare-bottom is especially common with Blue Diamond keepers focused on showing or breeding, since it makes the water-quality discipline this strain rewards much easier to maintain and lets you spot waste immediately against a plain bottom.
  • Water chemistry: Standard discus range — pH 6.0–7.2, 82–86°F, 2–8 dGH — but keepers chasing top-tier color tend to run tighter, more stable parameters than they might risk with a hardier patterned strain, since chemistry swings show up in the color first.
  • Lighting: Moderate, even lighting shows off the solid color better than heavy blue-spectrum aquascape lighting, which can wash out or oversaturate the blue tone under photos and in person alike.
  • Background: A dark background (black acrylic, painted glass, or simply a dark wall behind the tank) makes the metallic blue pop dramatically more than a bright or patterned background — a cheap, high-impact upgrade for a display-focused Blue Diamond tank.

Feeding

Standard discus feeding applies — a high-quality discus pellet as the staple, supplemented with beef heart mix or frozen bloodworms for growth and color, following the same schedule and portions covered in the feeding guide. Carotenoid- and spirulina-inclusive foods are commonly credited by breeders with helping intensify blue tones, though genetics and consistently clean water do the majority of the work — no food fixes a Blue Diamond kept in poor conditions.

Temperament & Compatibility

No different from other discus strains behaviorally — peaceful, shoaling, happiest in groups of six or more, and fully compatible with the same Amazon-biotope tankmates covered in the Red Turquoise guide (cardinal tetras, corydoras, rummynose tetras, otocinclus). Mixing strains in one display tank is common and doesn't cause behavioral issues; a tank of Blue Diamond alongside Red Turquoise and Pigeon Blood makes for a striking, high-contrast display that's a popular choice for show tanks specifically because the three strains grade so differently from one another.

Breeding Notes

Standard discus pairing and spawning behavior applies unchanged — see the breeding guide for the full breakdown of pair formation, conditioning, spawning, and the parental slime-feeding stage. What's different with Blue Diamond is the genetics behind the color: breeders selecting for solid color specifically cull for pattern-free offspring across multiple generations, so a home breeding project between two nice-looking Blue Diamond adults won't reliably reproduce show-quality solid color in the fry — some offspring will regress toward more patterned or patchy coloring. Serious color-line breeding is a multi-generation project best left to dedicated breeders unless that's specifically the project you want to take on.

Common Issues

Fading or Patchy Color

Cause: Almost always a water-quality or stress issue rather than illness — a missed water change, a new fish disrupting the group hierarchy, an undersized group, or a chemistry swing.

Fix: Test water immediately and check group dynamics for one fish being singled out. Color should recover within days once the stressor is identified and resolved; persistent fading despite good water and a settled group is worth a closer look for underlying illness.

Barring That Won't Fade

Cause: Can be genetics (incomplete color-line selection in that particular fish's lineage) or a sign of chronic low-grade stress that never fully resolves.

Fix: Rule out husbandry first — stable temperature, consistent water changes, adequate group size. If barring persists in an otherwise thriving, well-kept fish, it's likely just that individual's genetics.

Beyond these, Blue Diamond is subject to the same general discus health issues — hole-in-the-head, stunting, bloat — covered in the Red Turquoise guide's Common Issues section and in common diseases.

Where to Get Quality Fish

Because grading is so visible, Blue Diamond is a strain where buying from a reputable, lineage-transparent source matters more than most — a patchy or barred specimen is much harder to disguise at point of sale than it would be on a patterned strain. Look for even color with no patchiness, clean fins, and confident, undamaged posture, and ask about lineage if quality matters to you. Pet Zone SD stocks Blue Diamond when available, sourced and acclimated rather than rushed to the sales floor. See our sourcing guide for more on evaluating a seller.

Cost: Blue Diamond typically runs in the same $20–40 range as Red Turquoise for quality juveniles, though top-graded, exceptionally even specimens command a premium over more patchy or barred individuals of the same size.

Final Thoughts

Blue Diamond is a rewarding strain for a keeper who already has the discus fundamentals down and wants a fish that visibly reflects good husbandry back at them — there's nowhere for a solid strain to hide poor water or a bad diet. It's not the easiest first discus, but it's an excellent second or third one. Read the Red Turquoise guide first if you're new to the species, then come back here once you're ready for a strain that holds you to a higher standard.

Blue Diamond FAQ

What makes a Blue Diamond discus high quality?

Even, edge-to-edge metallic blue with no patchiness, dulling, or bleed-through of the underlying wild-type barring, plus clean fins and confident posture. Because there's no pattern to distract from flaws, any unevenness is immediately visible — which is exactly why grading is stricter for solid strains than patterned ones.

Is Blue Diamond a good beginner discus?

Not the best first strain. Care requirements are identical to any discus, but solid color shows stress, poor diet, and declining water quality faster and more visibly than a patterned strain — better suited to a keeper who already has the fundamentals down.

Why is my Blue Diamond discus turning pale or blotchy?

Almost always a water-quality or stress issue rather than illness — a missed water change, a new fish disrupting the group hierarchy, or a chemistry swing. Test water immediately; color should recover within days once the stressor is resolved.

Can Blue Diamond discus be kept with other discus strains?

Yes — mixing strains in one display tank is common and causes no behavioral issues. Blue Diamond alongside Red Turquoise and Pigeon Blood is a popular high-contrast combination.
