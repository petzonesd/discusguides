Blue Diamond Discus

By Pet Zone SD Team • September 2026

The Essentials
  • Size: 6–8 inches (15–20 cm) as adults
  • Tank: 55 gallons minimum for a group, tall (20"+ height)
  • pH: 6.0–7.2
  • Temperature: 82–86°F (28–30°C)
  • Hardness: Soft to moderate (2–8 dGH)
  • Temperament: Peaceful, shoaling
  • Difficulty: Intermediate to advanced — solid color shows stress and poor water fastest

Overview

Blue Diamond is one of the benchmark solid-color discus strains — bred for an even, near-metallic blue that covers the entire body with minimal secondary patterning. It's a genuinely different breeding project from patterned strains like Red Turquoise: where turquoise-and-red strains are judged on contrast and pattern definition, solid strains like Blue Diamond are judged almost entirely on evenness, saturation, and the absence of blemishes. That makes it an unforgiving strain to grade, and a satisfying one to keep well.

The trade-off for that clean, uniform look is that solid-color strains tend to show stress, poor diet, and declining water quality faster and more visibly than patterned fish — a stressed Blue Diamond dulls and blotches in a way that's immediately obvious, where a patterned strain can hide the same stress longer. Treat that visibility as a diagnostic advantage: this fish will tell you when something's wrong before it becomes serious.

Tank Setup & Water

  • Tank: Same baseline as Red Turquoise — tall, 55 gallons minimum for a growing group, more for mature adults.
  • Substrate: Bare-bottom is especially common with Blue Diamond keepers focused on showing or breeding, since it makes the water-quality discipline this strain rewards much easier to maintain.
  • Water chemistry: Standard discus range, but keepers chasing top-tier color tend to run tighter, more stable parameters than they might risk with a hardier patterned strain.
  • Lighting: Moderate, even lighting shows off the solid color better than heavy blue-spectrum aquascape lighting, which can wash out the blue tone.

Feeding

Standard discus feeding applies — high-quality discus pellets as a staple, supplemented with beef heart mix or frozen bloodworms for growth and color. Carotenoid- and spirulina-inclusive foods are commonly credited with helping intensify blue tones, though genetics and water quality do the majority of the work.

Temperament & Compatibility

No different from other discus strains behaviorally — peaceful, shoaling, happiest in groups of six or more. Mixing strains in one display tank is common and doesn't cause behavioral issues; a tank of Blue Diamond alongside Red Turquoise and Pigeon Blood makes for a striking, high-contrast display.

Breeding Notes

Standard discus pairing and spawning behavior — see the Red Turquoise guide for the full breakdown of pair formation, spawning, and the parental slime-feeding stage, which applies identically here. Breeders selecting for solid color specifically cull for pattern-free offspring, so a home breeding project won't reliably reproduce show-quality solid color without careful stock selection across generations.

Where to Get Quality Fish

Because grading is so visible, Blue Diamond is a strain where buying from a reputable, lineage-transparent source matters more than most. Look for even color with no patchiness, clean fins, and confident, undamaged posture. See our sourcing guide for more.

Final Thoughts

Blue Diamond is a rewarding strain for a keeper who already has the discus fundamentals down and wants a fish that visibly reflects good husbandry back at them. It's not the easiest first discus, but it's an excellent second or third one — read the Red Turquoise guide first if you're new to the species.
