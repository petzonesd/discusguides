Red Melon Pigeon Blood Cross Discus

By Pet Zone SD Team • September 2026

The Essentials
  • Size: 6–8 inches (15–20 cm) as adults
  • Tank: 55 gallons minimum for a group, tall (20"+ height)
  • pH: 6.0–7.2
  • Temperature: 82–86°F (28–30°C)
  • Hardness: Soft to moderate (2–8 dGH)
  • Temperament: Peaceful, shoaling
  • Difficulty: Intermediate to advanced

Overview

Red Melon Pigeon Blood Cross is exactly what the name describes — a hybrid line developed by crossing the warm, saturated color of Melon Red onto the pale, pattern-reduced Pigeon Blood base. The result is a strain that carries Pigeon Blood's translucent, light ground color with red patterning laid over it, rather than Pigeon Blood's more typical sparse red stippling.

As a cross strain, expect more variation between individual fish than you'd see in a longer-established, more standardized line like Red Turquoise or Blue Diamond — that variability is part of the appeal for keepers who like a strain that hasn't been fully "locked in" yet.

Tank Setup & Water

No different from other discus strains — see the Red Turquoise guide for the complete tank, substrate, and water chemistry breakdown. The pale base color makes water-quality issues (tannin staining, general dullness) show up clearly, so it doubles as a useful visual indicator of tank cleanliness.

Feeding

Standard discus diet — quality pellets as a staple, with beef heart mix and carotenoid-rich foods in rotation to support red saturation, per the feeding guide.

Temperament & Compatibility

Standard discus shoaling behavior — peaceful, group-dependent, happiest in six or more, with no special compatibility concerns beyond the usual discus tankmate guidance.

Breeding Notes

Standard pairing, spawning, and parental slime-feeding — see the breeding guide for the full walkthrough. Because this is a cross rather than a fixed line, fry from a home pairing will show a wider spread of color and pattern expression than fry from either parent strain bred pure — some closer to Melon Red, others closer to Pigeon Blood.

Common Issues

Sharing Pigeon Blood's pale base, this strain shows tannin staining and general water-quality decline more visibly than darker-bodied strains — treat yellowing or dulling as a maintenance signal first. Beyond that, it's subject to the same general discus health issues covered in common diseases.

Where to Get Quality Fish

Look for clean, unblemished coloring and confident, undamaged posture — the same checks that apply to Pigeon Blood, since this strain shares its pale base. Pet Zone SD stocks this cross when available. See our sourcing guide for more.

Cost: Typically $20–40 for quality juveniles, similar to its parent strains.

Final Thoughts

A good pick for a keeper who likes the pale, high-contrast Pigeon Blood look but wants more red warmth layered on top — the care is identical to any other farm-strain discus, the payoff is a genuinely distinctive hybrid look.

Red Melon Pigeon Blood Cross FAQ

What two strains make up Red Melon Pigeon Blood Cross discus?

It's a hybrid crossing Melon Red's warm, saturated color onto the pale, pattern-reduced Pigeon Blood base — combining Pigeon Blood's translucent ground color with red patterning laid over it rather than Pigeon Blood's typical sparse red stippling.

Why does this strain vary more than others in appearance?

As a cross rather than a long-established, standardized line, individual fish show more variation in exact color and pattern balance than a strain like Red Turquoise or Blue Diamond that's been bred toward a fixed look for decades.

Is Red Melon Pigeon Blood Cross discus hard to care for?

No — care is identical to any other discus strain. The pale base does make it a useful visual indicator of water quality, since tannin staining or dulling shows up clearly against the light ground color.
