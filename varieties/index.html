Discus Variety Library

Complete profiles for every major discus strain and wild type.

Unlike most cichlid genera, discus isn't really a "pick your species" hobby — nearly everything in the trade descends from a handful of wild populations that selective breeding has pushed in wildly different directions. Below are the strains and wild types worth knowing, in the order most keepers actually encounter them.

The Essential Three

Symphysodon spp. — farm strain

Red Turquoise Discus

The most popular strain in the hobby — turquoise base streaked with red, farmed by the millions and available at every skill level.

Tank: 55g+ min • pH: 6.0–7.4

Symphysodon spp. — farm strain

Blue Diamond Discus

Solid, near-metallic blue from head to tail. One of the cleanest "showpiece" solid-color strains and a benchmark for quality.

Tank: 55g+ min • pH: 6.0–7.4

Symphysodon spp. — farm strain

Pigeon Blood Discus

Pale, almost translucent body with red eyes and freckled red spotting. Polarizing, unmistakable, and a strong seller.

Tank: 55g+ min • pH: 6.0–7.4

Popular Strains

Snakeskin Discus

Fine, netted body patterning that looks etched rather than painted. One of the more distinctive patterned lines.

Tank: 55g+ min • pH: 6.0–7.4

Blue Snakeskin Discus

The same fine netted pattern as Snakeskin, expressed over a cool blue base instead of red or gold. One of the store's most popular strains.

Tank: 55g+ min • pH: 6.0–7.2

Marlboro Red Discus

Deep, solid red across the entire body with almost no secondary pattern. One of the most demanded solid-color strains.

Tank: 55g+ min • pH: 6.0–7.4

Rose Red Discus

A softer, pinker cousin of Marlboro Red within the same solid-red breeding tradition.

Tank: 55g+ min • pH: 6.0–7.2

Melon Red Discus

A warmer, brighter orange-red solid strain — part of the Melon family, close cousin to Marlboro Red.

Tank: 55g+ min • pH: 6.0–7.2

Checkerboard Discus

A broken, tiled body pattern that reads almost geometric. Less common at retail, popular with pattern collectors.

Tank: 55g+ min • pH: 6.0–7.4

Color & Pattern Variants

Fuji Apple Discus

Vivid red facial blush fading to a paler body, named for the apple variety it resembles.

Tank: 55g+ min • pH: 6.0–7.2

Melon Discus

Glowing solid orange-red, with Yellow Melon and Red Melon color forms. Close cousin to Marlboro Red.

Tank: 55g+ min • pH: 6.0–7.2

White Diamond Discus

Solid pearl-white with normal dark eyes — the non-albino counterpart to the Albino strain.

Tank: 55g+ min • pH: 6.0–7.2

Lemon Yellow Discus

Bright, clean yellow concentrated toward the face, closely related to Yellow Pigeon color forms.

Tank: 55g+ min • pH: 6.0–7.2

Yellow Pigeon Discus

Vivid yellow built on the same pigeon pattern gene as Pigeon Blood. Also sold as Yellow Checkerboard.

Tank: 55g+ min • pH: 6.0–7.2

Red Melon Pigeon Blood Cross

A hybrid strain crossing Melon Red's warm saturation onto Pigeon Blood's pale, translucent base.

Tank: 55g+ min • pH: 6.0–7.2

Blue Turquoise Discus

Brilliant near-solid electric blue with red dorsal accents — distinct from the heavily red-patterned Red Turquoise.

Tank: 55g+ min • pH: 6.0–7.2

Albino Discus

A recessive trait expressed across several strains — pale, translucent body and telltale red or pink eyes.

Tank: 55g+ min • pH: 6.0–7.2

Golden & Sunshine Discus

Vivid, saturated solid orange-gold, with normal dark eyes distinguishing it from the pastel albino form.

Tank: 55g+ min • pH: 6.0–7.2

Wild Type & Heirloom

Heckel Discus (Symphysodon discus)

The original — the only discus with a truly diagnostic dark third bar. Rio Negro blackwater specialist, still mostly wild-caught.

Wild Blue, Brown & Green Discus

The Symphysodon aequifasciatus/haraldi complex that founded every farm strain above. Subtle, hardy, and increasingly rare in the trade.

Choosing Your Strain

First discus? Start with Red Turquoise. It's the most widely farmed, most forgiving of the color strains, and gives you a full sense of what discus keeping actually involves before you spend more on rarer stock.

Building a show tank? Blue Diamond and Marlboro Red are the benchmark solid-color strains — judged hardest on evenness and saturation.

Chasing something rarer? Wild-type Heckel and the Blue/Brown/Green wild complex are a different hobby entirely — smaller, subtler, far less forgiving of poor water, and increasingly hard to source responsibly.

Farm Line Matters As Much As Strain Name

Two "Red Turquoise" discus from different farms can differ enormously in body depth, finnage, and hardiness. Asian farm lines (Malaysia, Thailand, Singapore) dominate the affordable end of the market and are generally the most forgiving for beginners. German and other European lines are typically pricier, more selectively bred for specific standards, and often the source of genuinely new strains. We'll flag lineage differences throughout these guides where they change how you should keep the fish.

Discus Varieties FAQ

What's the most popular discus strain?

Red Turquoise, by a wide margin — it's farmed by the millions across Southeast Asia, making it the most affordable and consistently available strain at every skill level.

What's the easiest discus strain for beginners?

Red Turquoise is the usual recommendation — the most widely farmed and most forgiving of the color strains. All discus share the same core water and feeding demands regardless of strain, so "easy" is relative within a genuinely intermediate-to-advanced hobby.

What's the rarest discus strain?

Wild-caught Heckel Discus and the wild Blue, Brown & Green complex are the rarest and hardest to source responsibly — smaller, subtler fish from specific Amazon blackwater regions rather than farm-bred color strains.

Are wild discus different from farm-bred discus?

Yes — every farm strain descends from the wild Symphysodon aequifasciatus/haraldi complex, but wild fish are subtler in color and far less forgiving of poor water than modern farm lines bred for tolerance.
