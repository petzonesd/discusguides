Discus Variety Library

Complete profiles for every major discus strain and wild type.

Unlike most cichlid genera, discus isn't really a "pick your species" hobby — nearly everything in the trade descends from a handful of wild populations that selective breeding has pushed in wildly different directions. Below are the strains and wild types worth knowing, in the order most keepers actually encounter them.

The Essential Three

Symphysodon spp. — farm strain

Red Turquoise Discus

The most popular strain in the hobby — turquoise base streaked with red, farmed by the millions and available at every skill level.

Tank: 55g+ min • pH: 6.0–7.4

Blue Diamond
Symphysodon spp. — farm strain

Blue Diamond Discus

Solid, near-metallic blue from head to tail. One of the cleanest "showpiece" solid-color strains and a benchmark for quality.

Tank: 55g+ min • pH: 6.0–7.4

Pigeon Blood
Symphysodon spp. — farm strain

Pigeon Blood Discus

Pale, almost translucent body with red eyes and freckled red spotting. Polarizing, unmistakable, and a strong seller.

Tank: 55g+ min • pH: 6.0–7.4

Popular Strains

Snakeskin Discus

Fine, netted body patterning that looks etched rather than painted. One of the more distinctive patterned lines.

Tank: 55g+ min • pH: 6.0–7.4

Marlboro Red Discus

Deep, solid red across the entire body with almost no secondary pattern. One of the most demanded solid-color strains.

Tank: 55g+ min • pH: 6.0–7.4

Checkerboard Discus

A broken, tiled body pattern that reads almost geometric. Less common at retail, popular with pattern collectors.

Tank: 55g+ min • pH: 6.0–7.4

Wild Type & Heirloom

Heckel Discus (Symphysodon discus)

The original — the only discus with a truly diagnostic dark third bar. Rio Negro blackwater specialist, still mostly wild-caught.

Wild Blue, Brown & Green Discus

The Symphysodon aequifasciatus/haraldi complex that founded every farm strain above. Subtle, hardy, and increasingly rare in the trade.

Choosing Your Strain

First discus? Start with Red Turquoise. It's the most widely farmed, most forgiving of the color strains, and gives you a full sense of what discus keeping actually involves before you spend more on rarer stock.

Building a show tank? Blue Diamond and Marlboro Red are the benchmark solid-color strains — judged hardest on evenness and saturation.

Chasing something rarer? Wild-type Heckel and the Blue/Brown/Green wild complex are a different hobby entirely — smaller, subtler, far less forgiving of poor water, and increasingly hard to source responsibly.

Farm Line Matters As Much As Strain Name

Two "Red Turquoise" discus from different farms can differ enormously in body depth, finnage, and hardiness. Asian farm lines (Malaysia, Thailand, Singapore) dominate the affordable end of the market and are generally the most forgiving for beginners. German and other European lines are typically pricier, more selectively bred for specific standards, and often the source of genuinely new strains. We'll flag lineage differences throughout these guides where they change how you should keep the fish.
