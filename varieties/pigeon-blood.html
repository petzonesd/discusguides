Pigeon Blood Discus

By Pet Zone SD Team • September 2026

The Essentials
  • Size: 6–8 inches (15–20 cm) as adults
  • Tank: 55 gallons minimum for a group, tall (20"+ height)
  • pH: 6.0–7.2
  • Temperature: 82–86°F (28–30°C)
  • Hardness: Soft to moderate (2–8 dGH)
  • Temperament: Peaceful, shoaling
  • Difficulty: Intermediate to advanced

Overview

Pigeon Blood is the most polarizing strain in this list, and it earns that reputation honestly. The base body is pale, almost translucent white or off-white, marked with red eyes and a scatter of red spotting — a look derived from the "pigeon" pattern gene originally developed in a related strain lineage. Keepers either find it strikingly clean and unusual, or too washed-out compared to the saturated color of Red Turquoise and Marlboro Red. There isn't a wrong answer; it's a real matter of taste.

Genetically, the pigeon pattern trait is linked to reduced pattern and pigment expression rather than a health defect — a well-bred Pigeon Blood is exactly as robust as any other farm strain. That said, because the trait has been used as a base for several derivative strains, quality and consistency vary more between breeders than with more established solid or patterned lines.

Tank Setup & Water

No different from other discus strains — see the Red Turquoise guide for the full tank size, substrate, and water chemistry breakdown. The pale body does make this strain a good visual indicator of tank cleanliness: any yellowing or dulling from tannin buildup or subpar water shows up clearly against the light base color.

Feeding

Standard discus diet — high-quality pellets as a staple, supplemented with beef heart mix, bloodworms, and live foods for conditioning. No special dietary requirements beyond what any discus strain needs.

Temperament & Compatibility

Identical shoaling behavior to other discus — peaceful with tankmates, occasional hierarchy-establishing chasing within the group. Mixes well visually and behaviorally with other strains in a display tank.

Breeding Notes

Standard discus pair formation and parental slime-feeding — see the Red Turquoise guide for the full breeding walkthrough. Because the pigeon trait's inheritance pattern is more variable than solid-color genetics, home-bred fry from a Pigeon Blood pair won't reliably all express the parent pattern.

Where to Get Quality Fish

Look for clean, unblemished pale coloring and vivid, clear red eyes — cloudy or dull eyes are a quicker health tell on this strain than on darker-bodied fish. See our sourcing guide for general buying advice.

Final Thoughts

Pigeon Blood is a strain worth choosing on aesthetics rather than any care difference — it keeps exactly like any other farm-strain discus. If the pale, red-flecked look appeals to you more than saturated color, it's a completely sound choice for a first or second discus.
