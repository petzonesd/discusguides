Ghost Discus

By Pet Zone SD Team • November 2026

The Essentials
  • What it is: A spontaneous 1988 mutation of Brown-strain wild-derived discus, lacking body bars
  • Not the same as: White Diamond (normal dark eyes, solid pearl body) or Snow White (no bars anywhere, including the eye)
  • Show class: Class 14 "Open," alongside Calico and Yellow/Red White — not Class 13 "White"
  • Size: 6–8 inches (15–20 cm) as adults
  • Tank: 55 gallons minimum for a group, tall (20"+ height)
  • pH: 6.0–7.2
  • Temperature: 82–86°F (28–30°C)
  • Hardness: Soft to moderate (2–8 dGH)
  • Difficulty: Intermediate to advanced

Overview

Ghost discus is one of the hobby's genuinely pattern-less strains — no vertical stress bars across the body at all — but it isn't quite the blank canvas its name suggests. Unlike Snow White, a Ghost fish can still carry partial barring on the eye and tail even with a bar-free body, and its eyes read as mostly white with a black pupil, sometimes carrying a faint tinge of red pigment. That eye coloring is the single most reliable tell for the strain, since it's a structural trait that doesn't fade in and out with the fish's mood the way normal discus "fright" darkening does.

Where It Comes From — and How It Differs From Snow White

A published discus-strain timeline (compiled from a Brooklyn Aquarium Society history and cited in discus-club literature) dates Ghost discus to 1988, describing it plainly as "a mutation of the Brown is discovered" — a spontaneous loss-of-pattern mutation found in ordinary Brown wild-type stock, not a trait someone deliberately crossed toward. That origin story puts it in different company than most of the strains covered elsewhere in this library: Red Turquoise, Marlboro Red, and the Pigeon Blood family all trace to breeders selectively intensifying a color or pattern they were actively chasing. Ghost's blank look was found, not bred for, at least at the start.

Snow White is the strain Ghost gets confused with most, since both are built on a pale, low-pattern body. The distinction hobby show judges actually use is the eye and face: a correctly bred Snow White shows no striations anywhere — body, face, or eye — and is judged in Class 13 "White" alongside white Pigeon Blood. Ghost is judged in the separate, catch-all Class 14 "Open," grouped with other mixed or non-standard patterns like Calico and Yellow/Red White, precisely because it doesn't meet the fully bar-free standard Snow White does. If you're buying specifically for one strain or the other, check the eye and tail before the body — that's where the two actually diverge.

This same point already comes up in the breeding guide's discussion of a 2022 discus genetics study: Ghost was one of the commercial strains found to be genetically close to its wild ancestors, with the visible differences between named strains coming down to phenotype — how genes express — rather than distinct breeding lines. Ghost is a clean example of that: one mutation, re-discovered or maintained in multiple farm lines, rather than a strain built up over generations of targeted selection the way Blue Diamond or Marlboro Red were.

Tank Setup & Water

No different from any other discus — see the Red Turquoise guide for the full tank size, substrate, and water chemistry breakdown (55 gallons minimum, tall tank, pH 6.0–7.2, 82–86°F). Ghost's pale, low-contrast body makes stress darkening and water-quality dulling easy to spot early, the same practical upside pale strains like White Diamond and Pigeon Blood carry.

Feeding

Standard discus diet applies without modification — a quality discus pellet as the staple, supplemented with beef heart mix, frozen bloodworms, and live foods for conditioning, per the feeding guide. Diet has no bearing on the bar-free trait itself, which is structural rather than diet-responsive.

Temperament & Compatibility

Identical shoaling behavior to every other discus strain — peaceful with tankmates, with the usual pecking-order chasing in a young group that settles as fish mature. See the social dynamics guide for group sizing and introducing new fish to an existing group.

Breeding Notes

Standard discus pair formation, spawning, and parental slime-feeding apply — see the breeding guide for the full walkthrough. Like most single-mutation pattern traits in discus, bar-free expression doesn't breed with simple, predictable inheritance; a home spawn from two Ghost parents can still produce fry with varying amounts of partial barring rather than a uniform bar-free litter.

Common Issues

Confusing a Stressed, Pale Discus for a Genuine Ghost

Cause: Any discus can look washed-out when stressed, newly shipped, or settling into a new tank, and a pale, low-contrast fish of an unrelated strain can superficially resemble Ghost or Snow White at a glance.

Fix: Check the eye, not just the body. A true Ghost's mostly-white, black-pupiled eye is a fixed structural trait that doesn't change with stress; a stressed fish of a normally dark-eyed strain keeps its normal eye color even while its body color temporarily dulls. If a "Ghost" you're evaluating has normal dark eyes, it's very likely just a pale or stressed individual of another strain, not Ghost.

Where to Get Quality Fish

Ghost is less commonly stocked at general retail than White Diamond or the mainstream solid-color strains, since its subtle, pattern-less look is a harder sell at a glance than a bold solid color. It shows up more often through specialty discus breeders and importers familiar with show-class terminology than through mass farm production aimed at casual buyers. Pet Zone SD can special-order Ghost-strain stock through its usual import channels — ask about current availability. See our sourcing guide for general buying advice.

Final Thoughts

Ghost is worth knowing by name even if you never keep one, because it's exactly the kind of strain that gets mislabeled in both directions — sold as "Snow White" by sellers who don't distinguish the two, or dismissed as a faded, low-quality fish by buyers who don't know a bar-free body is the point. Keeping requirements are identical to every other discus strain; the only real skill involved is in evaluating the fish itself before you buy.

Ghost Discus FAQ

Is Ghost discus the same strain as Snow White?

No, though they're frequently confused since both lack body bars. Ghost discus can still show partial bars on the eye or tail, while a correctly bred Snow White shows none at all — on the face, body, or eye. They also sit in different show classes: Ghost falls under the catch-all Class 14 "Open," while Snow White is classed under Class 13 "White."

When was Ghost discus developed?

A published discus-strain timeline places its discovery in 1988, as a spontaneous mutation found in ordinary Brown-strain discus — not a cross bred toward a pale look on purpose, the way several other pale strains were deliberately developed.

How do you tell a real Ghost discus from a stressed, faded fish of another strain?

Check the eye. A genuine Ghost keeps its mostly-white eye with a black pupil (sometimes a faint red tinge) whether the fish is calm or startled, because the lack of pigment is structural. A stressed or recently shipped discus of a patterned strain may look washed out body-wide, but its eye color and any bar pattern it does carry stay normal — a true Ghost's defining trait doesn't come and go with stress.

Does Ghost discus need any different care than other strains?

No. Like every other farm-strain color variant, Ghost's lack of pigment is a structural trait, not a sign of fragility, and it keeps on exactly the same tank size, water chemistry, and feeding routine as any other discus.
