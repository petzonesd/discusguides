Heckel Discus (Symphysodon discus)

By Pet Zone SD Team • September 2026

The Essentials
  • Size: 6–8 inches (15–20 cm) as adults
  • Tank: 75 gallons+ recommended, tall (24"+ height)
  • pH: 5.0–6.5 — genuinely soft, acidic blackwater
  • Temperature: 84–88°F (29–31°C)
  • Hardness: Very soft (0–3 dGH)
  • Temperament: Peaceful, shoaling, notably shyer than farm strains
  • Difficulty: Advanced — expert only

Overview

Symphysodon discus is the species that gave the entire genus its common name, and it's the most demanding fish on this list by a wide margin. Unlike the farm-bred strains that dominate the hobby, Heckel discus is still substantially wild-caught, imported from Rio Negro tributaries in Brazil — genuine blackwater habitat, stained dark by tannins and among the softest, most acidic natural water any commonly kept aquarium fish comes from.

The defining field mark is the "Heckel bar" — a noticeably darker, wider third vertical bar running through the middle of the body, distinct from the other, thinner bars. This is the one reliable way to separate true Heckel discus from the blue/green/brown wild-type complex covered separately, which lacks this emphasized central bar.

Wild Heckel discus are noticeably shyer and more skittish than farm-raised strains, which have been selectively bred for generations toward calmer, more outgoing temperaments around people and lights. Expect a wild Heckel to take much longer to settle, and to remain a less interactive display fish even once acclimated.

Tank Setup & Water — Why This Is a Different Hobby

Everything about keeping farm-strain discus gets more extreme with Heckel:

  • Water hardness: Genuinely soft water — under 3 dGH — is not optional the way it's a nice-to-have with farm strains. RO or rainwater blended to the correct chemistry is standard practice, not an advanced technique reserved for breeders.
  • pH: Held low, typically 5.0–6.5, mimicking the tannin-stained blackwater of the Rio Negro system.
  • Tank size: Bigger and taller than farm strains need — 75 gallons or more for a proper group, both for the fish's comfort and to buffer water chemistry swings, which hit harder in a smaller volume.
  • Décor: Driftwood and dim, indirect lighting mimic the shaded, structurally dense blackwater habitat and help a naturally shy fish settle.

Feeding

Wild-caught Heckel discus can be notoriously slow to accept anything but live food initially — bloodworms and live blackworms are the standard bridge food to get a new import feeding reliably before gradually introducing frozen and prepared foods.

Temperament & Compatibility

Shoaling like all discus, but shyer and quicker to spook. Best kept in a species tank or with only the calmest, least boisterous soft-water tankmates — cardinal tetras and Corydoras remain reasonable choices, but avoid anything active or attention-grabbing that might keep a wild Heckel perpetually startled.

Breeding & Conservation Notes

Wild Heckel discus are bred in captivity by specialists, but it's a genuinely different undertaking from breeding farm strains — the water chemistry demands that apply to keeping the fish apply even more strictly to successful spawning and fry rearing. Given wild collection pressure on specific river systems, buying only from importers and breeders who can speak to sourcing and sustainability is worth taking seriously with this species specifically.

Where to Get Quality Fish

This is not a fish to buy on impulse from general retail. Seek out specialist importers or breeders who can confirm collection origin, quarantine history, and feeding status. See our sourcing guide for general buying principles, and expect to pay a significant premium over farm-strain prices.

Final Thoughts

Heckel discus is the fish to work toward, not the fish to start with. If you haven't already kept and bred a farm strain like Red Turquoise successfully for at least a year, the jump to genuine blackwater chemistry and a shier wild fish is a steep one. For the keeper who's ready, it's as close as the hobby gets to the fish that started it all.
