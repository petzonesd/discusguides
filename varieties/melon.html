Melon Discus

By Pet Zone SD Team • September 2026

The Essentials
  • Size: 6–8 inches (15–20 cm) as adults
  • Tank: 55 gallons minimum for a group, tall (20"+ height)
  • pH: 6.0–7.2
  • Temperature: 82–86°F (28–30°C)
  • Hardness: Soft to moderate (2–8 dGH)
  • Temperament: Peaceful, shoaling
  • Difficulty: Intermediate to advanced

Overview

Melon discus is a solid-color strain built around a warm, glowing orange-red base — visually closer to the flesh of a cantaloupe or a ripe melon than the deeper brick-red of Marlboro Red, though the two are frequently confused and sometimes sold under overlapping names. Like the other solid-color strains, it's judged on evenness and saturation rather than pattern, and shows stress or poor water quality quickly and visibly.

The strain comes in a couple of recognized color forms: Yellow Melon (this page's photo), running toward a brighter, warmer yellow-orange, and Melon Red, pushed further toward red and overlapping meaningfully with Marlboro Red and Rose Red genetics (see the Marlboro Red guide for more on that overlap). Both forms are cared for identically — see the dedicated Melon Red guide for that color form specifically.

Tank Setup & Water

Requirements track the full Red Turquoise guide's tank, substrate, and hardscape breakdown.

  • Tank: Standard discus baseline — tall (20"+), 55 gallons minimum for a growing group of four to six juveniles, moving toward 75–125 gallons as adults.
  • Substrate: Bare-bottom is common among breeders and show keepers chasing peak color, same logic as other solid-color strains.
  • Water chemistry: Standard discus range — pH 6.0–7.2, 82–86°F, 2–8 dGH; stable, low-nitrate water helps maintain color saturation over time far more than any feeding trick.

Feeding

Standard discus diet — quality pellets as the staple, with beef heart mix and carotenoid-rich foods in rotation to support the warm orange-red tone, following the routine in the feeding guide. Diet supports what genetics and water quality already provide; it can't compensate for either.

Temperament & Compatibility

Standard discus social behavior — peaceful, shoaling, best in groups of six or more, fully compatible with the usual Amazon-biotope tankmates (cardinal tetras, corydoras, rummynose tetras, otocinclus). Makes a striking warm-toned contrast alongside cooler-toned strains like Blue Diamond or Blue Turquoise in a mixed display.

Breeding Notes

Standard discus pairing, spawning, and parental slime-feeding — see the breeding guide for the full cycle. As with other solid-color strains, achieving consistent, show-quality saturation across a whole spawn takes selective culling over generations rather than happening automatically, and fry can vary in exact tone even from well-matched parents.

Common Issues

Like any solid-color strain, Melon dulls or patches visibly under poor water, thin diet, or chronic stress well before a patterned strain would show the same problem — a useful diagnostic if you're watching for early trouble. Check water parameters and water-change frequency first; beyond that, it's subject to the same general discus health issues covered in common diseases.

Where to Get Quality Fish

Look for even, glowing color with no patchy or faded sections, and confirm with the seller whether you're looking at a Yellow Melon or Red Melon form if that distinction matters to you. Pet Zone SD stocks Melon color forms when available. See our sourcing guide for general buying guidance.

Cost: Typically $20–40 for quality juveniles, in line with other common solid-color farm strains.

Final Thoughts

Melon is a strong pick for a keeper who wants a warm, glowing solid-color centerpiece without necessarily committing to the deepest reds of a Marlboro — a satisfying strain that rewards the same water-quality discipline every solid-color discus demands.

Melon Discus FAQ

What color forms does Melon discus come in?

Two recognized forms: Yellow Melon, running toward a brighter, warmer yellow-orange, and Red Melon (Melon Red), pushed further toward saturated orange-red and overlapping meaningfully with Marlboro Red and Rose Red genetics.

Is Melon discus the same as Marlboro Red?

No — they're frequently confused and sometimes sold under overlapping names, but Melon and Marlboro Red are distinct breeding lines. Melon runs warmer, closer to cantaloupe orange; Marlboro Red is bred for a deeper brick-red.

Is Melon discus good for beginners?

It's a reasonable second or third discus rather than a first one. Care requirements are the same as any discus, but as a solid-color strain it shows stress and poor water quality faster and more visibly than a patterned strain like Red Turquoise.
