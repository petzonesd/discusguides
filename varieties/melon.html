Melon Discus

By Pet Zone SD Team • September 2026

The Essentials
  • Size: 6–8 inches (15–20 cm) as adults
  • Tank: 55 gallons minimum for a group, tall (20"+ height)
  • pH: 6.0–7.2
  • Temperature: 82–86°F (28–30°C)
  • Hardness: Soft to moderate (2–8 dGH)
  • Temperament: Peaceful, shoaling
  • Difficulty: Intermediate to advanced

Overview

Melon discus is a solid-color strain built around a warm, glowing orange-red base — visually closer to the flesh of a cantaloupe or a ripe melon than the deeper brick-red of Marlboro Red, though the two are frequently confused and sometimes sold under overlapping names. Like the other solid-color strains, it's judged on evenness and saturation rather than pattern, and shows stress or poor water quality quickly and visibly.

The strain comes in a couple of recognized color forms: Yellow Melon, running toward a brighter, warmer yellow-orange, and Red Melon, pushed further toward red and overlapping meaningfully with Marlboro Red and Rose Red genetics (see the Marlboro Red guide for more on that overlap). Both forms are cared for identically.

Tank Setup & Water

  • Tank: Standard discus baseline — tall, 55 gallons minimum for a growing group.
  • Substrate: Bare-bottom is common among breeders and show keepers chasing peak color, same logic as other solid-color strains.
  • Water chemistry: Standard discus range; stable, low-nitrate water helps maintain color saturation over time.

Feeding

Standard discus diet — quality pellets as the staple, with beef heart mix and carotenoid-rich foods in rotation to support the warm orange-red tone.

Temperament & Compatibility

Standard discus social behavior — peaceful, shoaling, best in groups of six or more. Makes a striking warm-toned contrast alongside cooler-toned strains like Blue Diamond or Blue Turquoise in a mixed display.

Breeding Notes

Standard discus pairing, spawning, and parental slime-feeding — see the Red Turquoise guide for the full cycle. As with other solid-color strains, achieving consistent, show-quality saturation across a whole spawn takes selective culling over generations rather than happening automatically.

Where to Get Quality Fish

Look for even, glowing color with no patchy or faded sections, and confirm with the seller whether you're looking at a Yellow Melon or Red Melon form if that distinction matters to you. See our sourcing guide for general buying guidance.

Final Thoughts

Melon is a strong pick for a keeper who wants a warm, glowing solid-color centerpiece without necessarily committing to the deepest reds of a Marlboro — a satisfying strain that rewards the same water-quality discipline every solid-color discus demands.
